Privacy

No Cookies, No Analytics, No Ads

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics, carries no ads and makes no requests to anyone else's servers. There are no accounts, no trackers and no logs of my own, because there is no server here to keep them.

Every site says something like that. What follows is the specific version, including the parts that are less flattering, and the way to check each claim rather than take my word for it.

What the site does not do

  • No cookies. Not one, of any kind. Nothing here writes document.cookie, and there is no server to set one.
  • No analytics. No Google Analytics, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo or anything else. No page-view counter, no session recording, no heatmaps. I genuinely do not know how many people visit.
  • No advertising and no third-party embeds. No ad network, no tag manager, no share buttons, no A/B tooling, no chat widget, no consent banner — a banner would only be needed if there were something to consent to.
  • No third-party requests at all. No CDN-hosted fonts, no icon library, no JavaScript framework fetched from someone else's domain. Every byte comes from this one domain. Even the favicon is drawn inline in the page rather than fetched.
  • No accounts, no sign-in, no email capture. There is nothing to register for.

This is enforced, not merely promised. The page is served with a Content-Security-Policy that permits resources from this origin and nowhere else. If a tracking script were ever added — by me, by accident, or by someone who got at the repository — your browser would refuse to run it. That is a stronger guarantee than a paragraph of text, because it does not depend on my continued good intentions.

What is stored in your browser

Two small things, in localStorage, both of them settings you chose yourself:

  • Your theme — whether you picked light, dark or auto.
  • Your country, and a city if you named one — so the list opens where you left it instead of asking again.

That is the complete list. It never leaves your browser, because there is nowhere for it to be sent: the published site is a handful of static files with no backend, no database and no API. Clearing site data in your browser erases all of it, and the site works exactly as well without it.

Location

The Near me tab can sort venues by distance. If you use it, your browser asks your permission first, and the coordinates it returns stay inside the page — the venue list is already loaded, so the distances are worked out on your own device. Your position is never transmitted, never stored, and is gone when you close the tab.

If you decline, or never ask, the site guesses a country from your timezone and language settings, which your browser already offers to every page without being asked. That guess is a country name and nothing more — never a position, never an address, and nothing precise enough to say which city you are in. You can overrule it with the country picker.

What I can see, and what I cannot

I want this section to be accurate rather than impressive, so: I collect nothing, but I am not the only party involved in getting these files to you.

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages and fronted by Cloudflare's free CDN. Like every web host on earth, they handle the request itself, which necessarily includes your IP address, and they keep their own operational logs under their own policies — GitHub's and Cloudflare's. I do not have access to those logs, I have never looked at aggregate traffic figures, and nothing in that data is tied to anything you did on the page. But it would be wrong to claim that literally nobody observes that a request happened.

That is the floor for any website that is not run off your own machine. What I can promise is that nothing has been added on top of it.

Links that leave this site

Venue rows link out to Google Maps, to a Google search for showtimes, and sometimes to the theatre's own website. Those are ordinary links: nothing is requested from Google until you click one, and no script from them runs here. Once you follow a link you are on their site under their rules, which are not these ones.

Showtimes are linked rather than stored, incidentally, for a licensing reason rather than a privacy one — but the effect is the same, in that nothing about what you searched for is sent anywhere on your behalf.

Getting in touch

There is no contact form and no email address on this site, which means there is nothing here that collects yours. Reports go to one of two places, both of them public and neither of them mine to hold:

  • Wrong venue data — a projector, screen size or location — belongs on the IMAX Wiki, where this site gets it from. Fixing it there reaches everyone rather than just this page, and flows back here on the next sync. It needs no account.
  • A broken link or a bug in the page goes to the issue tracker, which does need a GitHub account — that requirement is what keeps the queue readable.

Whichever you use, you are on their site under their privacy policy, not this one. I see what anybody else reading a public wiki page or issue thread sees, and nothing more.

How to check all of this yourself

None of the above should be taken on trust, and none of it needs to be:

  • Open your browser's developer tools, Network tab, and reload. You will see requests for this page, one stylesheet, three small scripts and one JSON file of venue data — every one of them from findmaxscreen.com. There is no other domain in the list.
  • Look at the Application or Storage tab. Cookies: empty. Local storage: the theme and country described above, or nothing at all if you have not changed either.
  • View source. The site is deliberately small and unminified. The whole thing is roughly 49 KB compressed, and it is meant to be readable.
  • Read the source repository. Everything that builds and publishes this site is public at github.com/findmaxscreen/findmaxscreen, including the deploy workflow, so you can see there is no step where anything is added.

Changes

If this ever stops being true, this page changes first and the change is visible in the repository's history. There is no version of this site where a tracker arrives quietly.
